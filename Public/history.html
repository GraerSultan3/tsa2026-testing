Unity through Education

The establishment of the town’s Texas A&M University took place in 1876 to provide Bryan residents access to higher education through agriculture and mechanical disciplines. Since, the University has grown from 40 students to over 500,000 alumni, nearly 20,000 who return to Bryan such as Dr. John A. Adams who has come back for Aggie Muster over 50 times. Aggie Muster is a community event that memorializes current or former students who have passed in the current academic year. To be remembered in a place you are meant to leave is a testimony to the unique bonds built through the education system Bryan is home to.

Unity through Equality

The Bryan Community is all encompassing to its citizens and to ensure equal opportunities and experiences, the people have created unique places to celebrate our diversity. In the 60s, City Council welcomed its first African American and Hispanic members, Harmon “Son” Bell and Anastacio “Andy” Herrera. The population includes a 60-20-20 split (Hispanic, Black, White), so since the promotion of diverse city leaders, social equality and interactions have been prioritized, displayed through the establishment of local organizations such as the Hispanic Forum, the Brazos Junior League, and the African American National Heritage Society. Established in 2000, the Hispanic Forum seeks to reach beyond any ethnic group and dedicates itself to scholarship to local students to attend a university of their choice. Although it is chapter of a national organization, JLB’s women are recognized statewide for developing the voluntarism and potential of young women as well as signature projects like Stuff the Bus; Stuff the Bus provides up to $70,000 annually in school supplies to the Bryan Independent School District. The African American National Heritage Society emphasizes the importance of African Americans story through America while offering a local frame in Bryan. The Brazos Valley African American Museum is fully maintained and was established by the AANHS to educate upon the history of African Americans in Bryan. Community outreach and involvement events include the Fiestas Patrias and Martin Luther King Jr. day that involve all residents from students to seniors.

Unity through Health

Community isn’t simply participating when the times are good, fun, or enjoyable; instead, the Bryan community has recognized that when people are facing the most desperate or scary time of their life, we must take care of the people we call our neighbor. Founded in 1984, the Brazos Valley Council on Alcohol and Substance Abuse has grown into an interactive non-profit organization seeking to aid in the “prevention, treatment, and recovery,” surrounding addiction in the greater Bryan area. This 501(c) 3 hosts shared partnerships with the school district, local healthcare, and small businesses to embark on a tiered approach towards health and wellness. Following its opening, nearly 2,000 Bryan citizens have taken part in residential programs on the road to sobriety.

Unity through the Next Generation

The evolution of excellence in the Brazos Valley is ceaseless; this made possible by the continuous investment from locals into our youth. The esteemed Brazos Valley Promise Program connects first-generation students as soon as their freshman year to prestigious universities and colleges. Advisory upon financial aid, tours of campuses, and multiple scholarships are available to all members of Promise to ensure equal access to post-secondary education. Additionally, admission to Texas A&M, the local university, includes a possible full ride scholarship to assist students in staying in town while gaining education to enrich their community.
